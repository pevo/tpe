## Day by day

### Part 1 — Taipei (Grand Hyatt, Xinyi)

**Tue Aug 4 — Arrival.** Land 8pm; immigration + bags with kids ≈ 60–90 min. Pre-booked van to the **Grand Hyatt** (~50 min), check-in ~10:30pm. Convenience-store snack run — there's a 7-Eleven on the block and the Taipei 101 basement next door. Grab EasyCards at the airport or any convenience store (~NT$500 loaded each; under-6s ride the MRT free).

**Wed Aug 5 — Gentle start.** Jet lag means 5am wake-ups — lean in: the **Xiangshan (Elephant Mountain) trailhead is a 15-minute walk from the hotel**. Dad + boys scramble the stone steps to the viewing boulders for sunrise over 101 (grandma sleeps in); back by 7:30. (Alternative: the Fuhang Soy Milk breakfast line, MRT to Shandao Temple before 7:30.) 8:30 red line two stops to **Daan Forest Park playground** (sand pit, slides) while it's cool. 11:00 Yongkang Street: **Din Tai Fung** — the Xinyi Rd flagship, or the Taipei 101 basement branch two minutes from the hotel if that queue is friendlier — then **Smoothie House mango shaved ice**. 1:30–4:30 hotel rest + **first pool session**. 5:00 red line direct to Shuanglian for **Ningxia Night Market** (the gentlest intro: compact, food-first): oyster omelet, Liu Yu Zi fried taro balls, papaya milk. Early night.

**Thu Aug 6 — Shilin kid HQ, then grandma's Songshan evening.** 8:30 red line direct to Jiantan (~25 min). 9:00 **Taipei Children's Amusement Park** (entry ~NT$30, rides NT$20–30, tap EasyCard). ### Part 2 — East coast (Dulan base, cousin's turf)

**Ground rule for the leg (Ghost Month + real currents): ocean swimming ONLY inside Shanyuan's roped, lifeguarded zone — guards on daily 10am–6pm. Dulan Beach and Jinzun are for walking, sandcastles, and views, never swimming.**

**Tue Aug 11 — Fly southeast.** 8:15 taxi to Songshan Airport (~20 min from Xinyi). Mid-morning flight, ~65 min — left-side windows for the Pacific coastline. Land ~noon, meet the cousin's van (or collect the rental — booking list #4), lunch in Taitung City (Mibanai for Amis indigenous cuisine — book ahead — or a beef-noodle joint). 2:00 **Taitung Forest Park**: shaded family bike loop around Pipa Lake (entry ~NT$30). 4:15 head up Highway 11 (~30 min) with a golden-hour stop at **Jialulan Recreation Area** — free ocean lawn, driftwood art, the boys' first Pacific run-around. 5:30 check in at the Dulan B&B and unpack for four nights. Lunch in Dulan; **12:45–3:00 rest block in the A/C**. 3:00 drive to Luye (~55 min), snack stop at the Luye 7-Eleven. 4:30 park at **Luye Highland** (lots fill early; small fee), spread a mat on the grass slope. **5:00–6:30 session:** dozens of balloons inflating and glowing at golden hour — Chiikawa, Hello Kitty and friends among them — tethered-ride queue if it's reasonable (~NT$500/person weekdays; weather-dependent, so frame it as a bonus, not a promise). **Thu Aug 13 — Grandma's family day · the four of us paddle.** Morning: drop grandma with the Taitung family for an unhurried day at their lead — the classic is **Mr. Brown Avenue** through the Chishang rice paddies + the famous railway lunchbox (Wu Tao or the Bento Museum; ~1 hr from Dulan). Back in Dulan by noon-ish; **1–4 rest block**. 5:30 early dinner around **Tiehua Music Village** (35 min), rejoining grandma + relatives. 7:00 — everyone together — the **night glow concert at Seashore Park's International Landmark: synchronized balloon burns, drone show, fireworks.** Free, open-air, bring the mat. *(SUP is guided flat-river paddling, not ocean swimming — but gut-check the Ghost Month comfort level with grandma before booking.)*

**Fri Aug 14 — Highway 11 north, last swim, farewell dinner.** 8:00 north up the coast (~45 min) while it's cool to **Sanxiantai**: free entry, NT$50 parking. Dad + boys cross the famous eight-arch footbridge (steep stairs — not for grandma); she beachcombs the pebble shore and hunts hermit crabs with whoever stays back. 10:30 return leg: **Donghe Bun Shop** (the classic Highway 11 snack stop, ~NT$25/bun) and a quick look at the **Jinzun overlook** (view only). Noon back in Dulan; **1–4 rest block, start packing for the north**. 4:00 **last Shanyuan swim** (lifeguards till 6, showers on site). 6:30 **farewell dinner with the cousin at the Dulan Sugar Factory** — craft stalls, Highway 11 craft beer for the adults; the signature live-music market is Saturday nights, but Friday sets happen — she's checking (booking list #4). ## Practical notes

- **Heat:** 90s°F and humid with afternoon thunderstorms. The 1–4pm indoor block is non-negotiable for grandma and honestly for everyone — on the coast leg it's A/C at the B&B, since ocean swims happen mornings and after 4pm.
- **Ghost Month (all 13 nights fall within it):** ocean swimming only inside Shanyuan's roped lifeguard zone, 10am–6pm — this is also just good water safety on a coast with real currents. Dulan Beach and Jinzun: feet-in-the-sand only. Brief the boys on the basics: chopsticks never upright in rice, don't touch roadside offerings, no whistling after dark.
- **Typhoons:** August is peak season. The festival pauses for weather; Taipei days are deliberately swappable; insurance covers the rest. If a storm threatens the east coast around Aug 11–15, swap in the Taipei spare blocks and shift the coast days later — flights and TRA rebook generously around typhoons.
- **Money:** night markets are cash — keep NT$2,000–3,000 in small bills on hand. 7-Eleven ATMs take US cards. No tipping anywhere. Dulan has a 7-Eleven and Family Mart but limited ATMs — cash up in Taitung City on arrival day.
- **Getting around Taipei:** MRT is elevator-equipped and stroller/senior-friendly everywhere; the hotel sits between Taipei 101/World Trade Center (red) and City Hall (blue) stations; never more than a NT$200–250 taxi if legs give out.
- **Pack:** swim caps ×5 · water shoes (rocky entries at Xiaoyeliu, hot sand at Shanyuan) · rash guards & sun hats · picnic mat (Luye + night glow) · motion-sickness bands for the Yangmingshan road · insect repellent for Luye and the coast at dusk · portable fans.
