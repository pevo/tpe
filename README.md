## Art hunting — one piece to bring home (per leg)

*The vacation-memento tradition, mapped to each base. Hours verified July 2026 — small venues shift, re-check the week of.*

**Taipei (Grand Hyatt, Aug 4–11):**
- **Apollo Building gallery cluster 阿波羅大廈** — Zhongxiao E Rd Sec 4, No. 218, ~10-min taxi (or blue line to Zhongxiao Dunhua). Taipei's original gallery building, still home to a dozen-plus dealers. Anchors: **Apollo Art Gallery 阿波羅畫廊** (2F, Tue–Sat 11–7; est. 1978 — Taiwanese senior masters and well-priced emerging painters, staff famously good at walking newcomers through) and **East Gallery 東之畫廊** (8F, Building B, Tue–Sat 12–6; modern Taiwanese painting and Hsu Tung Han's wood sculpture). **Both closed Sun–Mon** — best slot is a 1–4 rest-block substitution for the two of you (grandma covers the boys' pool hour) on Aug 5, 6, or 7.
- **Eslite Spectrum Songyan + Songshan Cultural & Creative Park** — daily 11am–10pm, and a 10-min walk from grandma's school, so it chains straight into the **Aug 6 evening**. Taiwanese artisan/craft floors (ceramics, woodwork, jade, prints), a basement art gallery, and glass-studio work — the "beautiful object" tier rather than gallery-wall tier, and the most kid-tolerant browse of the lot.
- *Logistics:* anything bought in Taipei rides the TSA→TTT flight with you — favor small/robust pieces or ask the gallery about shipping to the US (established galleries do this routinely).

**Dulan (Aug 11–15) — honestly the best art stop of the trip:**
- The village is the east coast's artist colony, and the **Sugar Factory 新東糖廠** is ground zero: the main warehouse art gallery (driftwood sculpture — Amis artist **Siki Sufin's** studio occupies warehouses 1–2), plus indie shops. Daily 8:30–6.
- **好的擺 handcraft art shop** (in the old supply warehouse) — Taitung makers' ceramics, woodwork, jewelry, prints. **Thu–Sun 11–5 only** → your window is Thu Aug 13 / Fri Aug 14.
- **台11 Highway 11 Open Studio** (under the big chimney) — painter Gao Min-shu's combined studio/select shop: her paintings, expressive hand-built ceramics, YAYA X MOUSSOU textile pieces; the craft-beer labels are her artwork too. **Fri–Sun 12–5 only** → Fri Aug 14, ~4:00, is *the* slot (already built into the day).
- **'Etolan Style 都蘭國 visitor hall** (5-min walk, daily exc. Wed 10–5) — Amis handcraft co-op, good for smaller pieces and the boys' souvenirs.
- *Logistics:* bring cash — small studios; and driftwood is a protected material handled by licensed local artists, which makes a small piece a genuinely of-this-coast memento.

**Qingpu finale (Aug 15–17):**
- **KIRI 國際原住民族文創園區 International Indigenous Cultural & Creative Park** — directly across Hengshan park from the playground, beside metro A17; 48 studio/shop spaces, ~80% indigenous-owned, plus the official 南島 select shop of juried indigenous work (weaving, wood, ceramics, textile art). Quiet on weekdays but **your two days are Sat–Sun — the market/performance days**, and it slots into the Aug 15 Hengshan golden hour.
- **Hengshan Calligraphy Art Center** (Wed–Mon 9:30–5, closed Tue; NT$100, seniors free) — museum rather than shop, but the museum store carries calligraphy prints and ink-art objects, and it's the architectural stunner of the district if Sunday midday needs an A/C block.

## Practical notes

- **Heat:** 90s°F and humid with afternoon thunderstorms. The 1–4pm indoor block is non-negotiable for grandma and honestly for everyone — on the coast leg it's A/C at the B&B, since ocean swims happen mornings and after 4pm.
- **Ghost Month (all 13 nights fall within it):** ocean swimming only inside Shanyuan's roped lifeguard zone, 10am–6pm — this is also just good water safety on a coast with real currents. Dulan Beach and Jinzun: feet-in-the-sand only. Brief the boys on the basics: chopsticks never upright in rice, don't touch roadside offerings, no whistling after dark.
- **Typhoons:** August is peak season. The festival pauses for weather; Taipei days are deliberately swappable; insurance covers the rest. If a storm threatens the east coast around Aug 11–15, swap in the Taipei spare blocks and shift the coast days later — flights and TRA rebook generously around typhoons.
- **Money:** night markets are cash — keep NT$2,000–3,000 in small bills on hand. 7-Eleven ATMs take US cards. No tipping anywhere. Dulan has a 7-Eleven and Family Mart but limited ATMs — cash up in Taitung City on arrival day.
- **Getting around Taipei:** MRT is elevator-equipped and stroller/senior-friendly everywhere; the hotel sits between Taipei 101/World Trade Center (red) and City Hall (blue) stations; never more than a NT$200–250 taxi if legs give out.
- **Pack:** swim caps ×5 · water shoes (rocky entries at Xiaoyeliu, hot sand at Shanyuan) · rash guards & sun hats · picnic mat (Luye + night glow) · motion-sickness bands for the Yangmingshan road · insect repellent for Luye and the coast at dusk · portable fans.
